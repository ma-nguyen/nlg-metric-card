## 2. Suitability of ROUGE-2 for Summarization  

### a. Strengths  
- **High change rate for non-summary injections**: Both injection types were penalized with "very high" change rates (-90.87% and -91.58%), demonstrating ROUGE-2's ability to detect outputs that lack semantic overlap with the source text.  
- **Consistency across injection types**: The metric produced nearly identical scores for both injection types, suggesting robustness to variations in phrasing.  

### b. Weaknesses  
- **Limited injection types tested**: The evaluation only included two injection types (affirmative statements instead of summaries). ROUGE-2's performance on other perturbation types (e.g., hallucinations, irrelevant content) remains untested.  
- **No explicit minimal score enforcement**: While the metric penalized injections heavily, it did not assign a score of 0, which could be critical for strict quality filtering.  

### c. Recommendation  
**Yes**. ROUGE-2 successfully detected the tested non-summary injections with "very high" change rates, aligning with human evaluation criteria.  

### d. Additional Improvements  
- **Expand injection types**: Test ROUGE-2 on diverse perturbations (e.g., hallucinations, redundant content) to validate generalizability.  
- **Incorporate semantic coherence**: ROUGE-2 focuses on n-gram overlap but may miss coherence or relevance. Hybrid metrics combining ROUGE with semantic similarity (e.g., BERTScore) could improve robustness.  
- **Define explicit minimal thresholds**: Establish a hard lower bound (e.g., 0.0) for non-summary outputs to align with strict quality requirements.  

---  
**Note**: While ROUGE-2 performs well in this controlled test, it may still fail to fully substitute human evaluation for complex perturbations or nuanced quality aspects.
