## 2. Suitability of **UniEval‑coherence(sum)** for **summarization**

### a. Strengths  
- **Injection 2** produced a *low* (‑13.36 %) change, indicating that the metric can penalise a non‑summary (a short “yes‑answer”) to some extent.

### b. Weaknesses  
- **Injection 1** received a *very low* positive change (+0.71 %), meaning the metric incorrectly rewarded a generic, non‑summary statement higher than the reference summary.  
- Neither injection achieved a *high* or *very high* change‑rate, suggesting limited discriminative power.  
- The metric does not consistently enforce the human‑expected ordering (perturbed < gold).

### c. Recommendation  
**No.** UniEval‑coherence(sum) does not reliably detect all injected non‑summaries with a sufficiently high change‑rate; one injection even scored higher than the gold reference.

### d. Needed improvements  
1. **Increase sensitivity** to textual relevance and factual alignment with the source document so that generic affirmative statements receive substantially lower scores.  
2. **Enforce a monotonicity constraint** ensuring that any perturbed (non‑summary) output never exceeds the gold summary’s score.  
3. **Calibrate the scoring range** to widen the gap between genuine summaries and off‑topic or filler injections, aiming for at least a *low* (10‑29 %) or higher negative change for all non‑summaries.  
4. **Incorporate source‑document grounding** more explicitly, perhaps via attention to content overlap, to better penalise injections that do not reflect the source.  

*Note:* Even with these enhancements, UniEval‑coherence(sum) would remain an automatic proxy and could not fully replace nuanced human evaluation.
