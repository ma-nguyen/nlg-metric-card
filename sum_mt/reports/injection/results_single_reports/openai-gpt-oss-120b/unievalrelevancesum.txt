**b. Were the scores similar for every injection?**

## 2. Suitability of UniEval‑relevance(sum) for summarization  

### a. Strengths  
*None of the injections produced a high (≥70 %) or moderate‑to‑high (50‑69 %) change rate. Consequently, the metric does not demonstrate a clear strength in penalising non‑summary perturbations.*

### b. Weaknesses  
- **Injection 1** – despite being a non‑summary statement, the metric increased the score by 15.3 % (low change).  
- **Injection 2** – the metric increased the score by 9.7 % (very low change).  

Both cases reveal that UniEval‑relevance(sum) fails to assign lower (penalised) scores to clearly irrelevant or fabricated “summaries,” contradicting the expected human‑like behaviour.

### c. Recommendation  
**No.** The metric does not reliably detect every injection (non‑summary) by producing a substantial negative change; instead, it erroneously rewards the perturbed inputs.

### d. Needed improvements  
1. **Calibration of relevance scoring** – the model should be fine‑tuned or re‑weighted to assign lower relevance scores to text that does not constitute a genuine summary of the source document.  
2. **Incorporation of factual consistency checks** – adding a component that verifies whether the candidate text is grounded in the source can help suppress scores for generic affirmative statements.  
3. **Training on adversarial examples** – exposing the metric to a broader set of injection types (e.g., boilerplate “Yes, …” statements) during training may improve its discriminative power.  
4. **Threshold optimisation** – establishing a minimal acceptable score for summaries could prevent the metric from awarding overly high values to non‑summaries.

*Note: Even with these enhancements, UniEval‑relevance(sum) may still fall short of fully substituting human evaluation, but the suggested adjustments are necessary to bring its behaviour closer to human judgments.*
