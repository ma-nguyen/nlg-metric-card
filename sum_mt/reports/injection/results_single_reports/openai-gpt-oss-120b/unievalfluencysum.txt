## 2. Suitability of **UniEval‑fluency(sum)** for **summarization**

### a. Strengths  
*No injection exhibited a high, moderate, or even low change‑rate.* Consequently, there are no observed strengths in terms of detecting non‑summary perturbations.

### b. Weaknesses  
- **Injection 1** – very low change‑rate (4.38 %).  
- **Injection 2** – very low change‑rate (4.64 %).  

Both injections, which are clearly not summaries of the source document, were scored **higher** than the gold summaries, violating the expected human‑like behavior that perturbed (non‑summary) outputs should receive lower scores.

### c. Recommendation  
**No.** The metric fails to penalize obvious non‑summary injections; it even rewards them with higher scores. Therefore, UniEval‑fluency(sum) is not currently suitable as an automatic substitute for human evaluation of summarization quality.

### d. Needed Improvements  
1. **Incorporate a penalisation mechanism** for outputs that do not reflect the content of the source document (e.g., minimal score floor for non‑summaries).  
2. **Enhance content‑relevance sensitivity** so that the model can distinguish between genuine summaries and generic affirmative statements.  
3. **Calibrate the scoring range** to ensure that gold summaries receive consistently higher scores than any injected non‑summary text.  
4. **Validate on a broader set of perturbations** (e.g., factual errors, hallucinations) to confirm that the metric reliably mirrors human judgments across diverse failure modes.

*Note: Even an ideal metric may not fully replace human evaluation, but it should at least align with the basic human expectations outlined above.*
