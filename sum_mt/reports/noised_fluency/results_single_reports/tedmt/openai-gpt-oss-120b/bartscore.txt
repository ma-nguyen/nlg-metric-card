**Explanation of the attributes**

* **Monotonous decrease** – the reported “Change %” becomes increasingly negative as the perturbation level grows. All tests satisfy this condition.
* **Superlinear decrease** – the absolute magnitude of the change grows faster in successive steps. This is observed for random word drop, preposition removal, stop‑word removal, truncate, and article removal. The other tests either show roughly constant increments or a single perturbed point, thus are not superlinear.
* **Change‑rate** – determined by the largest absolute change in the series:  
  * *low* ≤ 25 % (verb lemmatization, punctuation, sentence‑middle swap, preposition removal, article removal)  
  * *moderate* ≤ 60 % (stop‑word removal, random local swap, truncate, random token repetition)  
  * *high* > 60 % (random word drop).

## 2. Suitability of **bart_score** for **machine translation**

A metric is deemed suitable when **most > 7 perturbation tests** exhibit both a **high change‑rate** *and* a **superlinear decrease**.  

*Only one test* (random word drop) meets the “high change‑rate” criterion, and only five tests show superlinear behaviour. Consequently, the required condition is not satisfied.

**Recommendation:** **bart_score is *not* recommended** as a primary evaluation metric for machine‑translation quality, because its sensitivity is generally low to many perturbations and it lacks the strong, superlinear response that aligns with human judgments of fluency degradation.
