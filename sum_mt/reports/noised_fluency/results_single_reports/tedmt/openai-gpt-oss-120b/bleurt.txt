## 1. Sensitivity to the Perturbation  

| Perturbation Type | Monotonous Decrease | Superlinear Decrease | Change‑Rate |
|-------------------|---------------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Verb Lemmatization | yes | no | high |
| Word Removal | yes | no | high |
| Punctuation Noise | yes | no | high |
| Sentence‑Middle Swap | yes | no | moderate |
| Preposition Removal | yes | no | high |
| Stop‑Word Removal | yes | no | high |
| Random Local Swap | yes | no | high |
| Truncate (end‑word removal) | yes | **yes** | high |
| Article Removal | yes | no | high |
| Random Token Repetition | yes | no | high |

*Monotonous decrease* is marked “yes” when the reported change becomes increasingly negative as the variation level grows.  
*Superlinear decrease* is marked “yes” only for the truncate test, where the magnitude of the score drop accelerates with larger deletions.  
*Change‑Rate* follows the defined thresholds:  
- **low** ≤ 25 % drop,  
- **moderate** ≤ 60 % drop,  
- **high** > 60 % drop (or approaching the metric’s minimum).  

All perturbations exhibit a monotonic decline; only the truncate perturbation shows a super‑linear pattern. Every test except the sentence‑middle swap reaches a high change‑rate, with the swap test being moderate.

---

## 2. Suitability of **bleurt** for **machine translation**

A metric is recommended when **most > 7** perturbation tests display a **high change‑rate** **and** a **superlinear decrease**.  

- High change‑rate: 9 out of 10 perturbation families (all except the sentence‑middle swap) achieve a high change‑rate.  
- Superlinear decrease: only the truncate test shows superlinearity; the majority do **not**.

Because the superlinear criterion is not met for the majority of perturbations, **bleurt is *not* recommended** as a standalone metric for machine‑translation quality assessment, despite its strong sensitivity to many types of noise.
