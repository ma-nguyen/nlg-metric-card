## 1. Sensitivity to the Perturbation  

For each perturbation type we assess:

* **Monotonous decrease** – does the score consistently drop as the variation grows?  
* **Superlinear decrease** – does the magnitude of the drop accelerate with larger variations?  
* **Change rate** – based on the largest observed percentage change (low ≤ ‑25 %, moderate ≤ ‑60 %, otherwise high).

| Perturbation | Monotonous decrease | Superlinear decrease | Change rate |
|--------------|---------------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Verb lemmatization | yes | no | low |
| Word removal | yes | no | low |
| Punctuation noise | yes | **yes** | low |
| Sentence‑middle swap | yes | no | low |
| Preposition removal | yes | **yes** | low |
| Stop‑word removal | yes | no | low |
| Word swap | yes | no | low |
| Truncate (end removal) | **no** (score first improves) | no | low |
| Article removal | yes | **yes** | low |
| Token repetition | yes | no | low |

*Explanation of superlinear decisions*  
- **Punctuation noise**: the second step (‑3.3 %) is a larger drop than the first (‑1.5 %).  
- **Preposition removal**: the second step (‑3.5 %) exceeds the first (‑1.8 %).  
- **Article removal**: the second step (‑2.2 %) exceeds the first (‑0.9 %).  
All other perturbations show either constant or decreasing incremental drops, thus are not superlinear.

---

## 2. Suitability of **bart_score_avg_f** for **summarization**

A metric is deemed suitable when **most (> 7) perturbation tests** exhibit **high change rate** *and* **superlinear decrease**.  

- The present analysis shows **all** perturbation types have a **low** change rate.  
- Only three tests (punctuation noise, preposition removal, article removal) display superlinear behaviour, and none reach a high change rate.

**Conclusion:** *bart_score_avg_f is **not** a reasonable metric for evaluating summarization quality under the considered perturbations.* It reacts only weakly to increasingly severe degradations and fails to capture the superlinear penalisation that human judges apply to major fluency errors.
