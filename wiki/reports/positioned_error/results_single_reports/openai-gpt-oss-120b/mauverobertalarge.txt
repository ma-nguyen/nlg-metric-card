## 1. Sensitivity to the Perturbation  

**a. Did a perturbed output score higher than the gold output?**  
No. Every perturbed variant (both shuffling and random) received a lower score than the corresponding gold (reference) texts.

**b. Were the scores similar for every location (beginning, middle, end)?**  
No. The magnitude of the score drop varied considerably across locations:  
- For shuffling, the drop was smallest in the middle (‑36.7 %) and largest at the end (‑73.9 %).  
- For random perturbations, the drops were uniformly severe (‑87.6 % to ‑96.8 %) but still differed between middle (‑87.6 %) and the two extremes (‑96 %‑‑96.8 %).  

Thus, location influences the metric’s sensitivity.

**c. Were the scores similar for shuffling and random‑test?**  
No. Random perturbations produced far larger degradations (‑87 % to ‑96 %) than shuffling (‑36 % to ‑74 %). The metric is markedly more sensitive to the insertion of completely random tokens than to token reordering.

## 2. Suitability of **MAUVE‑RoBERTa‑Large** for **open‑ended text generation**

### a. Strengths  
- **High/Very‑high sensitivity** to severe semantic disruptions: random replacements at any position caused very high (‑87 % to ‑96 %) score reductions, indicating the metric can detect nonsensical token insertions.  
- **Consistent detection of end‑position perturbations**: shuffling at the end yielded a high drop (‑73.9 %), showing the metric is responsive when coherence is broken near the conclusion of a passage.  

### b. Weaknesses  
- **Moderate to low sensitivity to mild perturbations**: shuffling in the middle only reduced the score by 36.7 % (low to moderate), suggesting the metric may under‑penalize subtle reordering that still harms readability.  
- **Location‑dependent variability**: the metric penalizes beginning and end shuffles more than middle shuffles, which does not align with the human expectation that any incoherent segment should be equally detrimental.  
- **Insufficient granularity**: the metric does not reach a minimal floor (e.g., 0) even for completely random token blocks, limiting its ability to reflect the “minimal score” behavior observed in human judgments.  

### c. Recommendation  
**No.** The metric exhibits high or very‑high change rates in only **four** of the six perturbation variants (random‑beginning, random‑middle, random‑end, shuffling‑end). The threshold of > 5 high‑change variants required for a positive recommendation is not met.

### d. Needed Improvements  
1. **Increase sensitivity to mid‑sentence disruptions** – adjust the underlying distribution comparison to capture local coherence breaks, ensuring that shuffling anywhere in the text yields comparable penalties.  
2. **Introduce a lower bound** that approaches zero for completely meaningless token sequences, better mirroring the human “minimal score” scenario.  
3. **Normalize across positions** so that the metric’s response is less dependent on where the perturbation occurs, aligning more closely with human judgments that any incoherent segment is equally detrimental.  
4. **Validate against a broader set of perturbations** (e.g., lexical substitution, syntactic errors) to confirm that the observed patterns generalize beyond the specific shuffling/random tests.  

---

*While MAUVE‑RoBERTa‑Large demonstrates promising ability to flag gross semantic violations, its uneven sensitivity across perturbation types and locations limits its suitability as a full replacement for human evaluation in open‑ended text generation.*
