## 1. Sensitivity to the Perturbation  

**a. Did a perturbed output score higher than the gold output?**  
No. Every perturbed variant received a lower MAUVE‑GPT2‑base score than its corresponding gold (reference) text.

**b. Were the scores similar for every location (beginning, middle, end)?**  
No. The metric exhibited a strong dependence on where the perturbation was applied:  
- End‑perturbations caused a **very high** drop (≈‑98 % to ‑99 %).  
- Beginning and middle perturbations produced modest to moderate declines (‑13 % to ‑63 %).  

Thus, scores were not uniform across locations.

**c. Were the scores similar for shuffling and random‑test?**  
No. While both test types showed the largest degradation at the end, their behaviour elsewhere differed:  
- Shuffling‑middle yielded only a **low** drop (‑13 %), whereas random‑middle produced a **moderate‑to‑high** drop (‑63 %).  
- Beginning perturbations were also less severe for shuffling (‑42 %) than for random (‑33 %).  

The metric distinguishes between the two perturbation strategies.

## 2. Suitability of **MAUVE‑GPT2‑base** for **open‑ended text generation**

### a. Strengths  
- **End‑perturbations** (both shuffling and random) trigger a **very high** change‑rate (‑98 % to ‑99 %).  
  This indicates that the metric is highly sensitive when semantic coherence is destroyed at the tail of a passage, aligning with human intuition that such outputs are almost nonsensical.

### b. Weaknesses  
- **Beginning perturbations** (‑42 % and ‑33 %) fall into the *low‑to‑moderate* range, suggesting limited sensitivity to disruptions at the start of a text.  
- **Middle shuffling** shows only a *low* change (‑13 %), implying the metric may overlook substantial semantic damage occurring mid‑sentence.  
- **Middle random** yields a *moderate‑to‑high* drop (‑63 %) but still far from the very high degradation seen at the end, indicating inconsistent responsiveness across locations.  
- Overall, the metric’s scores vary considerably with the perturbation position, which may not reflect the uniform severity perceived by human judges.

### c. Recommendation  
**No.** The metric exhibits high/very‑high change‑rate in only **2** out of the 6 evaluated variants (both end‑perturbations). The threshold for a positive recommendation (> 5 variants with high or very‑high change) is not met.

### d. Needed Improvements  
1. **Enhanced sensitivity to early and mid‑sentence disruptions** – the metric should penalize incoherent beginnings and middle sections more strongly, matching human judgments that any loss of meaning, regardless of position, degrades quality.  
2. **Reduced location bias** – calibrate the scoring function so that comparable semantic damage yields comparable score drops, independent of where the perturbation occurs.  
3. **Broader perturbation coverage** – incorporate additional perturbation types (e.g., lexical substitution, syntactic reordering) during calibration to ensure the metric captures a wider spectrum of quality degradations.  
4. **Alignment with human rating scales** – perform a correlation study between MAUVE‑GPT2‑base scores and human Likert ratings on the same perturbed set to quantify residual gaps and guide further tuning.

*Even with these enhancements, MAUVE‑GPT2‑base should be used as a complementary signal rather than a full replacement for human evaluation in open‑ended text generation.*
