## 1. Sensitivity to the Perturbation  

**a. Did a perturbed output score higher than the gold output?**  
No. Every perturbed set received a lower score than the corresponding gold set.

**b. Were the scores similar for every location, meaning the scores of beginning, middle and end were similar?**  
No. Scores for perturbations at the **end** of the text dropped dramatically (≈ ‑47 %), whereas perturbations at the **beginning** and **middle** produced only marginal declines (‑0.4 % to ‑7.1 %). Thus, the metric is not uniformly sensitive across locations.

**c. Were the scores similar for shuffling and random‑test?**  
Only partially. For **beginning** and **middle** perturbations, both shuffling and random replacements yielded very low changes (‑0.4 % to ‑7.1 %). For the **end** location, both tests produced a large drop (‑46.9 % vs. ‑47.0 %), but the magnitude of change for the less‑severe perturbations differed (shuffling ≈ ‑0.4 % vs. random ≈ ‑7 %). Hence, the metric behaves similarly for severe end‑perturbations but diverges for milder perturbations.

## 2. Suitability of **MAUVE‑RoBERTa‑Base** for **open‑ended text generation**

### a. Strengths  
- **Detects severe degradation**: When 10 consecutive tokens at the **end** are either shuffled or replaced with random tokens, the metric registers a substantial drop (≈ ‑47 %), classified as *low to moderate* change‑rate. This aligns with human intuition that such perturbations severely damage coherence and meaning.  

### b. Weaknesses  
- **Insensitivity to subtle perturbations**: For perturbations at the **beginning** or **middle**, the metric’s change‑rate is *very low* (‑0.4 % to ‑7.1 %). Human readers would still notice the incoherence introduced by shuffled or random token blocks, especially in the middle of a passage, yet the metric barely reflects this.  
- **Location bias**: The metric reacts strongly only when the disruption occurs near the **end** of the text, suggesting an over‑reliance on tail‑distribution statistics rather than a holistic assessment of semantic integrity.  
- **No high‑change‑rate categories**: None of the six perturbation variants reach the *high* (70‑89 %) or *very high* (90‑100 %) change‑rate thresholds that would indicate robust discrimination.

### c. Recommendation  
**No.** The metric exhibits high‑change‑rate behavior in **0** out of the 6 tested variants (the requirement is > 5). Consequently, it does not meet the stipulated criterion for recommending its use as a reliable proxy for human evaluation in open‑ended generation tasks.

### d. Needed Improvements  
1. **Increase sensitivity to local semantic disruptions** – incorporate token‑level or span‑level coherence checks so that shuffling or random insertion anywhere in the text yields a more pronounced score decline.  
2. **Balance positional influence** – adjust the underlying distributional comparison to treat all positions equally, mitigating the current end‑bias.  
3. **Calibrate change‑rate mapping** – refine the metric’s scaling so that modest but perceptible degradations (e.g., 5‑10 % drop) are classified as *low* rather than *very low*, providing clearer interpretability for practitioners.  
4. **Validate against broader perturbation families** – extend evaluation to synonym substitution, grammatical errors, and content omission to ensure the metric captures a wide spectrum of human‑perceived quality loss.  

*Even with these enhancements, MAUVE‑RoBERTa‑Base should be used alongside human judgments, as no automatic metric can fully replace nuanced human evaluation.*
