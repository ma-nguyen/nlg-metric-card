### 1. Sensitivity to the Perturbation  
**a.** No perturbed output scored higher than the gold output. All perturbations resulted in lower scores.  
**b.** Scores were not similar across locations. End perturbations showed significantly larger drops (-46.9% to -47.0%) compared to beginning (-0.4% to -7.1%) and middle (-1.4% to -5.3%) perturbations.  
**c.** Scores were not similar between perturbation types. Random replacements caused larger drops than token shuffling for beginning (-7.1% vs. -0.4%) and middle (-5.3% vs. -1.4%), but both types had nearly identical drops at the end (-47.0% vs. -46.9%).  

---

### 2. Suitability of MAUVE-RoBERTa-Base for Open-Ended Text Generation  
**a. Strengths**  
The metric showed **low-to-moderate** sensitivity (46.9–47.0% change rate) for perturbations at the end of texts (both shuffling and random). This aligns with human expectations that nonsensical endings should lower scores.  

**b. Weaknesses**  
The metric exhibited **very low** sensitivity (0.4–7.1% change rate) for perturbations at the beginning and middle. Token shuffling in these regions had minimal impact (-0.4% to -1.4%), and random replacements showed only marginal drops (-5.3% to -7.1%). This indicates insensitivity to nonsensical early/mid-text content.  

**c. Recommendation**  
**No**. Only 2/6 variations (end perturbations) achieved a change rate ≥30% (low-to-moderate). The remaining 4 variations fell below 10% (very low).  

**d. Improvements Needed**  
1. **Positional robustness**: Enhance sensitivity to perturbations at the beginning and middle of texts.  
2. **Minimal scoring**: Assign near-minimal scores (closer to 0) for outputs with severe semantic disruptions (e.g., random tokens), as the current end-perturbation scores (∼0.51) remain above the theoretical minimum.  
3. **Perturbation-type consistency**: Address the discrepancy between shuffling and random replacements in early/mid-text regions.
