## 1. Sensitivity to the Perturbation  

For each perturbation type we assess:

* **Monotonous decrease** – does the score consistently drop as the variation grows?  
* **Superlinear decrease** – does the magnitude of the drop increase with higher variation (i.e., later drops are larger than earlier ones)?  
* **Change rate** – classified as **low**, **moderate**, or **high** according to the largest absolute change (‑25 %, ‑60 %, otherwise).

## 2. Suitability of **mauve‑roberta** for **open‑ended text generation**

A metric is deemed suitable when **most (> 7) perturbation tests** exhibit both a **high change rate** *and* a **superlinear decrease**.  

From the analysis above, only **five** perturbation families (Word removal, Stop‑word removal, Random local swap, Truncate, Random token repetition) satisfy both conditions. The remaining families either have moderate/low change rates or lack a superlinear pattern.

**Conclusion:** **mauve‑roberta is *not* recommended** as a primary automatic metric for evaluating open‑ended text generation, because it does not consistently produce strong, superlinear sensitivity across a sufficient number of perturbation types.
